Rodolphus absorbed it in silence, and the cynicism in his hollow face slowly deepened into something like grim recognition.

Three-fifths.

He did not doubt it. That was the terrible thing—he did not doubt it for an instant. He knew these men. He had knelt beside them for a decade, had watched Yaxley calculate and Nott hoard and Malfoy buy his way out of Azkaban with gold he'd sworn he didn't have. He knew exactly how a pure-blood lord's professed devotion evaporated the moment his vault was threatened.

“Cowards,” Rodolphus said quietly. Not to her. To the room. To his entire generation. “A conqueror at the gates and half his lords are quietly wiring their gold into goblin escrow so they'll have something left when he burns.” His mouth curved without humor. “Bella would have killed every one of them herself if she'd known. She believed. She actually believed.

He was silent a moment, and something moved behind his eyes—the filing-away of a very dangerous piece of knowledge, that the goblins held the secret purse-strings of most of the Dark Lord's inner circle. But he was too far into his own ruin to do anything with it now except recognize its weight.

“Very well, Countess. You have explained Malfoy. He is one coward among many, and I will treat him as such—a nervous fool to be watched and reported on, nothing more.” He drew a slow breath. “I accept your terms. I will swear.”

Kharza's quill moved to a fresh line. Dradnok shifted forward.

But Rodolphus lifted one hand—not to stop them, but to add something, and his voice changed as he did, dropping into the low, careful register of a man offering a first coin of good faith to prove the rest are coming.

“And because I am about to bind my life to yours,” he said, “I will give you something now. Freely. Before the oath. So you understand the quality of what you are buying.” His hollow eyes fixed on the smoked glass. “You want to know what troubles the Dark Lord? What he does when he believes no one watches?”

The chamber went very still.

“Since Hogwarts—since the raid, since Dumbledore fell and Bellatrix vanished and half the raiding party never came home—he has changed. Not grief. He does not grieve. But he has grown… distracted.” His brow furrowed, genuinely baffled. “He is checking on his hidden possessions, Countess, over and over, like a man counting coins he suspects are being stolen—and whatever he is counting, the numbers are frightening him.”

The Morsglass-free skin of his forearm bore only the old Dark Mark, dull in the candlelight, as he extended his hand palm-up over the black table—ready to swear, and watching the mirror to see what his first gift had bought him.
